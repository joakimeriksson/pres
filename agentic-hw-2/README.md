# Hardware in the agentic loop 2 — web deck

Part two of [`../agentic-hw`](../agentic-hw/): the two emulators live, DeviceDock, and the intro to a half-day
hardware-in-the-loop hackathon for embedded developers. reveal.js 5, no build step to view, same look as part one.
24 slides in three parts after a short why (emulators, DeviceDock, your afternoon), 15:00 planned: `data-timing`
per slide sums to 900 s and the note time stamps are computed from it. Many slides are 30–40 s; dividers are 5 s.

## Run

    cd ..  &&  python3 -m http.server 8000      # serve the repo root, then open http://localhost:8000/agentic-hw-2/

Keys: `S` speaker view, `F` full screen, `D` reboot the demo on a demo slide, `↓` the extra scenario under a demo.

## Editing

`index.html` is generated by `build.py`, which takes part one's styles, its "agent grades its own tests" diagram and
the robot arm from `../agentic-hw/index.html` and adds the new slides. The board drawn wherever the story says "board"
is part one's IoT board (the one the arm presses), defined once as an SVG symbol `#devboard`. Edit `build.py`, then run it from the repo root:

    python3 agentic-hw-2/build.py

## Demos

* **esp32sim** (slide 7, extra: `c6-rpl-net`): `demos/esp32sim` is a symlink to `~/work/esp32sim/web` (git-ignored);
  on GitHub Pages the hosted `https://mikroverk.github.io/esp32sim/` is used instead.
* **Cooja-NG** (slide 9, extra: `mixed`): always the hosted WebAssembly build,
  `https://mikroverk.github.io/cooja-ng/player.html?wasm=<scenario>`; scenarios are listed in its `scenarios.json`.
* Both are cross-origin on Pages, and Cooja-NG always: once you click inside a demo, use the ↻ ‹ › buttons in the
  caption bar. The arrow keys are forwarded only from a same-origin demo (esp32sim served locally).
